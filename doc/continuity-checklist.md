# Phone/computer continuity acceptance

Use the same new PR3 build on Android and the computer. These are manual acceptance steps, not claims of completed physical-device testing. The first release supports one continuity peer per device, chosen items, and foreground sync.

1. Connect both devices to the same trusted local network. In the computer's PR3 data-folder `pr3.toml`, keep existing settings and set `[node]` to `serve = true`, `listen = "0.0.0.0:7313"`; restart PR3. The default loopback address serves simulators only. Allow PR3's incoming connection in the OS firewall if prompted. Use the computer's actual LAN IP on the phone (not `0.0.0.0`, `127.0.0.1` or the emulator address).
2. Pair in **Settings → Devices** using the computer's address and one-time code. Verify continuity is enabled automatically on both devices, with no separate switch, including after restarting an already-paired installation. Pairing alone must not copy unselected items. Leave both apps open; the phone syncs immediately after pairing and every 15 seconds while visible, or tap **Sync now / retry** there.
3. Save and confirm a harmless memory on the phone. Choose **Share with paired device**. Verify it appears on the computer and that editing there is refused. Correct it on the phone, sync, and verify only the corrected fact is recalled on both. Unselected/proposed facts must remain local.
4. Open a task with a short conversation on the phone. From its More menu choose **Move to paired device**. Verify the phone cannot Continue/Finish/Retry it, even after restart. Sync, then Continue on the computer and confirm the earlier goal/details are present. Move it back and resume on the phone.
5. Repeat with a task that was waiting for email approval before restarting the source app. The destination must require a fresh request/review and device approval; it must not open the mail app during sync. A task with an uncertain email outcome must keep that warning and have no one-click retry. Existing email handoffs still do not attest to sending or delivery.
6. Disconnect after a Move, restart either app, reconnect and retry. Verify there is one editable owner and one task per device, with no duplicated chat entries or mail handoffs. Inspect the last exchange time, pending acknowledgements and failure message; no exchange must be reported successful before the database commit.
7. Disconnect one device, delete a shared task or Forget a shared memory on the other, then reconnect. Verify the other copy disappears and does not return after another restart/sync. A forgotten fact and a separate conversation are distinct records; delete the conversation separately if its text must also be removed.
8. For retained-copy recovery, **Unpair** (phone) or **Forget** (computer) the peer on the device that has a read-only replica. Choose **Recover a separate copy**, then confirm the second tap. Verify a private editable copy is created and the old shared identity disappears. Pair the same devices again and verify the old identity is deleted on the peer. Recovered tasks show **Outcome unknown** and have no one-click retry because the replica cannot attest to actions performed since its last sync. Review action results before explicitly requesting any new action.
9. Unpair/Forget the peer and verify continuity is disabled and subsequent sync is refused, including after restart. Pair the same devices again and verify sync resumes automatically. Copies on an unreachable/revoked device cannot be remotely erased; existing tombstones wait for reconnection and renewed pairing.

Recovery here is local replica recovery, not encrypted backup/key recovery or replacement-device enrollment. Keep the existing device identity/database together; silently restoring an old ownership state or replacing a key would risk two writers. Multi-peer/replacement-device enrollment, system background sync, encryption at rest and formal distributed-model checks remain roadmap work.

Record Android model/OS, computer model/OS, app build, network, the time from Move/Delete to visible change, and any Needs attention message. No real email needs to be sent for these tests.

These results do not establish physical-device latency, provider delivery or the broader multi-seed model benchmark baseline.
